Blood Meridian

Blood Meridian is considered McCarthy’s masterpiece. It bathes in violence. An occasional refrain on the Reading McCarthy podcast is, “no author has killed as many babies as McCarthy.” This is so. One coming to McCarthy with only this perception, and perhaps the images of a burntout country and ash from The Road, they probably think the books are reveling in that violence. That is not so. The Kid comes out of Tennessee into the West and tumbles and starts problems until he falls in with Captain Glanton’s gang of scalphunters. Among that gang eventually are The Kid, Toadvine, Expriest Tobin, and several others. The Judge is the neonlit attraction. He arrives over and again from nowhere and leaves to nowhere and while he’s there he wanders around nude and glowing and pontificating. The Judge attracts a lot of attention from readers; maybe the expriest attracts just a little less. On this my second read, the expriest stood out the most and it is easy for the reader to imagine the Kid with the Judge on one shoulder and the Expriest on another whispering in his ears. ...

Suttree

It’s finally done, I’ve read everything that Cormac ever published. What a year. I plan to write something about that elsewhere, but for now, Suttree. I think this is Cormac’s longest novel at nearly 500 pages. It is episodic in nature and the episodes are presented without a lot of scaffolding to let you know. Cormac provides you with the changing of the seasons and that is the major progressive force of the novel. There is little plot other than Suttree’s sort of underboiling search for self. I think you’d have to read it a few times to really mine it, and I think there is something there to mine. ...

Child of God

McCarthy’s third novel. The one I’ve enjoyed the least. It is very dark, about someone with a sad life who descends into depravity. I read in two sittings, last Sunday and today. It feels like McCarthy still finding his form. The story is more or less a character study, following Ballard throughout his life. It’s difficult to say why I leave so uninterested in Ballard. It it not an interesting thing to be sad and lonely. Maybe the things that happen to a person to render them this way can be interesting, but watching a person descend was not for me. Ballard is dealt a bad hand and makes no decision to help himself, or even to not hurt others. He demonstrates little care for anyone, and is cold and isolated to the point of being a figment of nature. ...

The Counselor

One more down in the Year of Cormac. I am not surprised to be less than pleased with The Counselor. In my Letterboxd review of Ridley Scott’s adaptation (https://boxd.it/5bor6n), I lament the film’s incoherence. The screenplay is certainly more coherent, but I think it has some problems. Reading this let me understand why The Stonemason didn’t quite work for me, either. In Cormac’s novel/prose work, the entire construct belongs to his language. All facets of the world are crafted by it. The dialogue is enmeshed in the narration, thought, and description. All blends, and he is fantastic in this form. In a screenplay, you have stage direction and dialogue. Nothing else. It lacks a dimensionality on which McCarthy’s work depends. ...
