The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time

November 7, 2026 — Nintendo

Let’s say it is early 1999. I live in a town of 800 people, just off the “main” road. The house is a grey thing, a little formless to my memory. You enter to the kitchen, on the left is a bathroom, and then through an entryway is a living room. What would be a dining room, to my mind, is the bedroom. There are two other rooms on the ground floor, but for reasons beyond memory they are uninhabited and uninhabitable. In one of them are some boxes of stuff (I think toys and boxes and things like that); near the top is the box for the Nintendo 64 that’s on the living room floor. There’s one, and only one, cartridge for the system in the house: Super Mario 64.

But immediately across the street in a pink and baby blue trailer, there’s another Nintendo 64. And somewhere next to Jet Force Gemini and Yoshi’s Story and maybe Star Fox, there’s The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. “Grandma’s” is written on it in black magic marker. 28 years later, that cartridge is in Washington, DC, in something called an Analogue 3D, in my apartment. It looks like this:

I played a lot of Ocarina, though I played much more and have much deeper memory of Majora’s Mask which we rented (golden, holographic stickered cartridge) from a tiny little store that also sold Hunt Bros. Pizza. And yet, I never beat either of them. How’s that possible? I never beat them on the 3DS, either. For years I thought I had, until replaying it as an adult and realizing I’d just replayed the opening over and over again (probably because we’d been renting it). Similar is the case for Ocarina. Over and over for almost 30 years, I’ve played all the way up to the Forest Temple, and then gotten distracted. It’s the same for Twilight Princess, as well—I’d get to the Water Temple and lose interest (TP has never been my favorite, despite having the hottest Link). Wind Waker I’ve beaten countless times and it’s probably still my favorite in a few aspects (best combat, music). A Link to the Past—How many times have I played the first few dungeons? Dozens. Same for Oracle of Seasons.

How many Zelda games have I actually beaten? Wind Waker, Minish Cap, Link’s Awakening, Breath of the Wild, Tears of the Kingdom, and Echoes of Wisdom. And now Ocarina of Time.

When Nintendo announced the remake a few months ago, I felt cautiously excited. For a while I’d contemplated replaying the game with intention, I didn’t want my first time beating it to be on the remake, wouldn’t that be weird? So I turned on the Analogue 3D (which I purchased purely to play this very cartridge) and played a few hours a week for a while. I got into the Water Temple, then faced Dark Link, and got my shit kicked in. It was my fault: poor resource management, poor planning. The original Ocarina is not flush with resources, and you can find yourself run-low fast. Well, I got busy and by the time I got back, I was holding the Switch 2 remake in my hand.

Reader, I feel no shame of beating Ocarina as a remake first. What an accomplishment this thing is! When the trailer released, many expressed anger or worry about the graphics. I saw it compared to some of those Unreal Engine fan creations. This is nothing new. I remember holding Nintendo Power in my hands when they ran the first previews of Wind Waker, which I thought looked great, and so many detested the graphics—people wanted realistic. Well, whatever. Parts of this edition’s graphics do appear perhaps a little over-designed, but it does look fanciful, it looks like a fantasy. I like that.

It took me 22 hours and 45 minutes to beat the remake. For 28 years I’ve played Ocarina and for whatever reason, I couldn’t put it down this time. Not for whatever reason, I guess. These are the reasons:

  • The original Ocarina is a near-perfect game, you don’t have to beat it to know;
  • Modern camera controls do wonders for speed of gameplay;
  • Aiming the slingshot, bow & arrow, boomerang, hookshot, etc, is much improved, and the slight motion-aiming is a plus;
  • Items no longer de-spawn, so you’re rarely out of resources.

I like all of these changes. A few folks I know are playing at the same time wondered, do the changes make the game too easy? I’m not sure. There are elements that are less scary. I remember going to the forest temple for the first time when I was a kid and being scared of the Moblins in the maze. At least some of this fear is because the camera is difficult to control in the original, even while those controls were revolutionary at the time. One had to position themselves against a wall and turn and Z-target to look around corners. In 30 years, we’ve learned a lot about how to move a camera in the gameworld, and as a result the remake’s feel is smooth and fluid. It also means I killed all the Moblins in the maze immediately, which I did not know was even possible.

Another change that I noticed first in the trailer but experienced first in the Forest Temple, the Floormasters now cast a multicolored spotlight down on Link as they prepare to drop down. In the original, it was simply a growing black circle. Both feature music. Similar to the Moblins, these things scared the piss out of me as a kid. And a few months ago on a replay, they continued to scare me. Mostly because if you succumb to them, you’re sent back to the start of the dungeon, which is a big pain in the ass. I don’t think they do that in the remake, but I don’t know because I felt emboldened by the ability to movethe camera and as such whipped six shades of shit out of them.

My most-recent playthrough of the original paused in the Water Temple, which I tried to handle without a guide but failed. In the 3DS version, they’d made changes to this temple’s lighting to make it more navigable, and these are present in the remake as well. I still found it a little more thought-demanding than the other early temples, but in general I flew threw it.

Sometimes it is hard to know what I’ve played before and haven’t, because apart from playing parts of Ocarina for near-30 years, I’ve also read so much lore, watched playthroughs, lore videos, just so much! I love Zelda.

Ocarina also has a different thing for me because Adult Link was one of the first times when I recognized a guy as hot.

I don’t usually write reviews of games. Sometimes I’ll write something on Backloggd, but they’re halfhearted at best. But Zelda is a life-shaping thing, for me. It’s a world that I love and cherish. If I’m still doing anything in 30 years, it’ll probably still involve playing Zelda games. And that’ll have been because of Ocarina of Time. I have an emotional connection to these stories that I don’t always well-understand, but they’ve been part of my life since before I could read. So, perhaps it is not surprising that I teared up as the end-credits rolled over this lovely remake of an amazing game.
