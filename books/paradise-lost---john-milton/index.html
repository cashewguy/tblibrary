Review

Really good! I find that I do not have complicated thoughts. I bought this copy of The Portable Milton from Vintage at some long past point and it sat on my shelf for ages. Some time ago I removed it from my shelf and, decided I’d never read it, put it on the “to little free library” table. By some cosmic chance I then saw the painting Pandemonium by John Martin:

Looks sick, no? So I picked the book up on my way to work and went to reading.

I found it took a while to read, but is another book helped by reading out loud. When I read that Milton, blind, dictated the book, that made a lot of sense to me. It reads very well. There are some more or less boring parts (the final Book in particular did little for me), but the stuff in Hell and Eden is pretty good.

You know who gets a bad rap in this tale? Eve. Geez, as soon as she eats that apple they really treat her like shit. Everything is woman’s folly, I mean they’re a step away from calling her a dopey mare. Clearly, no one was there to hear Satan, because he made good points! Similar to the Old Testament, God comes off as real twat.

Over and over, Adam and Eve (really, Adam, the angels rarely if ever deign to talk to Eve) are told more or less to mind their own business and just thank their lucky stars:

Solicit not thy thoughts with matters hid; Leave them to God above, him serve and fear; Of other creatures, as him pleases best, Wherever placed, let him dispose; joy though In what he gives to thee, this Paradise And thy fair Eve; Heaven is not for thee too high To know what passes there; be lowly wise: Think only what concerns thee and thy being; Dream not of other worlds, what creatures there Live, in what state, condition, or degree, Contended that thus far hath been revealed not of Earth only but of highest Heaven.
(page 419)

I would not respond well to this. If God gets to be a busybody knowitall, why can’t I?

I could write some longer more thoughtful review but there really isn’t any point, it’s Paradise Lost.
