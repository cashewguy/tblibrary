Review

Note: Rather annoyed to go looking for metadata on this book and find RAW:PReP, PEDAGOGY, AND THE POLITICS OF BAREBACKING which comes 10-years later and includes an afterward from Dean. While I later reference Hatred of Sex I will also have to read this and perhaps return to some of my critiques below.

Introduction

Tim Dean’s Unlimited Intimacy released in 2009, 28 years after the first CDC reporting of what would eventually be called AIDS, and 3 years before the FDA approved Truvada (PrEP) for pre-exposure prophylaxis. Its situation in time makes Unlimited Intimacy a little quirky. Reading in 2026, so many of the statements feel ancient, and the mindsets archaic. Such that generational differences cannot be put aside while considering the contents. Perhaps as a result of those generational differences, some of Dean’s contentions and conclusions feel strange and ill-considered. In particular, Dean’s paintbrush when it comes to men who bareback is obscenely wide—in some paragraphs he seems to suggest that anyone fucking without a condom is hard mostly as a result of gambling with HIV.

I’ve never read a book with so many citations to pornographic films. The titles are helpfully listed in a Videography. Some samples: Anal Cum Drippers, Breed Me, Fucking Crazy, Plantin’ Seed, Swallow and why not also, Cocktails #2? Glancingly, Dean will discuss commodification of sex, but not so much porn’s direct impact on sex itself. He is more interested in what porn says about the people viewing it, are we all perverts? Yes, certainly (if you are reading this and saying, well I’m not a pervert, I insist that you reflect on what gets you going a bit more). Do we all secretly want to be fucked by a group of people in a park in the middle of the night? No. Not that there’s anything wrong with that. Dean also titles a section, “Fucking as Research” which I find very funny, though the subject of said section is rather serious. Dean states that not a little of his primary research for the text happens in the bathhouses and gay bars oft explored in the book, and many is the time that Dean pulls dialogue and exchange directly from (his) in-progress sex encounters. I admire it, though am not sure my writing here will be quite so exposed, and if it were, it couldn’t be so academic.

It is worth noting that my approach to this book is as someone with formal training in social work, who is gay, and who has had sex with strangers (oh my!). My academic work did not focus on HIV and nor does my professional work. Dean, in the book, speaks a lot about seeking not to pathologize the practice of barebacking, and indeed to depathologize it, and this I align with.

On Language

Before I get to my critique, perhaps what I enjoy most about this book is the use of language. Over-academician descriptions of sex and the people that have it gross me out a bit. Men who have sex with men (MSM) as a term appears all over academic literature. It is meant to capture “not just gay” men who have sex with men. Sure, fine. Generally, though, academic literature depicts sex so dryly, clinically, that it almost seems inhuman.

I like that this book uses the word fuck when it is talking about people fucking. I like that it does not spend a lot of time trying to dress up what we all know involves sweat, spit, lube, cum, moaning and laughter. If you’re going to write a book entirely focused on barebacking, to approach it any other way would seem a farce.

In the closing chapter, Dean takes passing shot at “family-values”:

The family-values rhetoric that has achieved such prominence in contemporary U.S. political discourse endorses above all the importance of the familiar, whereas I wish to stress the value for democratic life of contact with the unfamiliar, even the strange.
(page 191)

I like this. I want to be more direct, which is that I consider the family-values rhetoric in the United States to be a corrosive rot which is destroying democratic foundations of our country and facilitating acts of violence towards anyone that does not look exactly like those espousing the family-values line. This is, perhaps, more apparent in 2026 than in 2009—but not really.

Bareback Conflation

The most serious problem with Dean’s book is its flattening of men who bareback into one giant pool of people who are either actively looking for, or apathetic towards getting, HIV. It must be stated that this book exists in a pre-PrEP world, in which the only real risk-mitigations were condoms and serosorting (which Dean describes along with a few other less effective strategies). Other writing will have to explore the impact of PrEP on risk seeking behavior. But I feel comfortable saying that (and even pre-PrEP) not all bareback sex can be described as risk-seeking behavior. Risk-taking, risk-apathetic, sure. While I will write more of what I view as generational differences between this book’s world and that of today, this problem I think exceeds major generational concerns.

Very early, Dean develops the image of a barebacker, and begins with an asterisk: any gay man may have unprotected anal sex without adopting the identity of a barebacker (page 10). However, as he continues on just after to cite posts on barebacking websites to define the population, he here and later begins to conflate having bareback sex, preferring bareback sex, and especially, actively seeking out HIV by means of anonymous bareback sex. Dean’s writing is such that it is hard for me to determine if this conflation is an intentional flattening of the milieu of men that fuck raw, or if it is just that such a flattening helps his case. Throughout the introduction I bumped on his apparent flattening of all men who fuck raw into a single subculture and his prescription of certain characteristics or attitudes onto that subculture. Despite the asterisk that Dean applies early, he does not consider seriously the idea of men who fuck bareback that aren’t part of the subculture. All are introduced and brought in either by virtue of psychoanalytic id-superego tango. My knowledge of the psychoanalytic framework is essentially nil and Dean does not describe it sufficiently to provide a reader that goes in without a familiarity with Lacan and Freud a good understanding of the approach. I do not necessarily fault Dean for this because, ultimately, this is an academic text published by an academic press, not pop non-fiction. Nonetheless, it leaves me finding his groupings of people suspect. Not until page 53 did I find an example of dubiousness:

Bug chasing should be understood as one of the more extreme forms of body modification. . . . The slang term “poz” (also the title of a long-established U.S. magazine for HIV-positive readers) functions as both noun and verb: to “poz” another guy is to ejaculate HIV-infected semen inside him. Hundreds of online profiles represent men who are clamoring to enact the fantasy of “getting pozzed.”(10) The term “poz” implies that infected semen has developed a positive rather than negative connotation in the subculture’s argot. . .

10. A random quantitative check of the Web site [sic] [url to a defunct site called ‘ultimate bareback’], on July 25, 2006, indicated 41,626 active members; a search under the category “bug chaser” yielded 726 profiles—that is, 726 members who, when creating their online cruising profile, clicked an icon to indicate that they were interested in giving or receiving “the bug.”

“Hundreds” is portrayed as a great mass of people, but “hundreds” is in fact not a great mass, even in the citation Dean deploys to strengthen the statement. He cites ‘Ultimate Bareback’ (a now defunct website specifically for people who bareback, one could not imagine a place more predisposed to Dean’s assertion of a subculture) and a random check thereof, showing 726 profiles tagged as interested in giving/getting HIV. Of course this does seem insane to the average person, who in their right mind would actively be seeking out HIV? Yet, those raised online may know that one can find anything and everything there. On this site for those who fuck raw, only 1.74% of users declare themselves as interested in giving or receiving “the bug.”

1.74% is not a number of substantial enough mass to declare that all men on UltimateBareback.com are interested in flirting with HIV, and even less so for all other men who prefer sex without a condom.

Speaking of such preferences, a little later in this same section Dean notes that research suggests the amount of men who enjoy barebacking in North America is “surprisingly large.” He has no citation for this, but why would he? If researchers found that every single person with a penis who also enjoys sticking it in things (or who has a receptacle they enjoy having penii into which stuck) found that those folks enjoyed fucking raw, even that would not be a surprisingly large amount. In the same section, the author discusses ‘gift-giving’—the practice of intentional infection of a pattern with HIV—and contemplates the proportion of men who like to fuck raw and how they do not seem to think of themselves as gift-givers. I’m not sure what could be surprising about this, especially when the universe being considered includes the HIV-negative and the HIV-status-unknown and status-apathetic.

Dean does make this note:

It needs to be acknowledged that a substantial proportion and perhaps the majority of instances of barebacking combine a desire for unprotected sex with a desire to contain HIV. Plenty of HIV-negative men practice unsafe sex while nonetheless wishing to remain uninfected; correlatively, most HIV-positive men who bareback have no wish to infect others. Yet only recently have statements such as these become intelligible as anything other than paradoxes or contradictions. . . . The willingness to take [sexual] risks has been accompanied by the development of various strategies of risk reduction; abandoning condoms does not mean abandoning all caution or sense of responsibility.
(page 12)

And yes, that does need to be acknowledged, because while Dean writes this, the rest of this book does not seem to consider it. Today, people should understand clearly:

It is possible to want to feel someone cum inside you without wanting to get HIV. It is possible to want to cum inside someone without wanting to give them HIV.

Unfortunately, I don’t think the book completely accepts that premise. Dean suggests that, always, on some level, HIV and the risk of it are interlinked with bareback sex. Maybe his is an understandable mentality for someone who came of age during the AIDS crisis, but I find it a vicious association that, in a world with accessible testing, PrEP, DoxyPEP, and hopefully better communication, serves no purpose.

The subculture Dean discusses is not as wide as simply “men who fuck raw.” It is more like, men who fuck raw while being apathetic to or interested in transmission of HIV, which is quite distinct from men who fuck raw generally. Generational Concerns

Through HIV, gay men have discovered that they can “breed” without women. *Unlimited Intimacy* does not take for granted what might seem obvious, namely, that bareback subculture is all about death. . . . barebacking also raises questions that complicate how we distinguish life-giving activities from those that engender death.
(page 6)

Many times throughout Dean’s writing I thought, “I have no connection to this statement at all,” and could not identify any memory of anyone saying anything (or not saying anything) similar to me. Bareback subculture is all about death? This statement must be a relic of that interregnum between the beginning of the AIDS crisis and the slow change of sexual dynamic brought about by easier access to HIV testing and PrEP. Elsewhere, Dean says that HIV never comes up in his fucking, which really blew me away. While anecdotal, nearly every anonymous encounter I’ve ever had has included (often at my initiation, though not always) what I consider typical questions of sexual hygiene. And when they didn’t, that was a notable thing that might cause anxiety later! If this statement was well-founded in 2006, or 1999, it is not in 2026.

Back to Dean. It’s also a little odd that “breeding” here is tied to “breeding” HIV, which is presented as analogous to breeding for the purposes of creating a child, though I do not think this is a well-founded analogy. It seems a clearly fetishistic thing tied to impregnation, which has commonality in straight and gay porn. To assert that the intentional transmission of HIV is an act of rebellion / towards creating a “child” in the form of the infected is reaching. Despite the obvious denotation of the word, I suspect the word “breeding” in the context of (what makes it effective in) porn and horny sex has little to do with procreation.

Speaking of breeding:

Apparently the heterosexuality of these men remains uncompromised by their unsheathed erections rubbing together to the point of orgasm inside an anus, because the anus belonged to a woman. The structure of homosociality—in which putatively straight guys bond with each other through the mediation of a female figure—can tolerate overt physical intimacy between men, provided there is a woman somewhere in the picture who functions as a guarantor of the sex-gender system. Even as the most extreme forms of straight porn come to approximate bareback gay porn, a barrier divides them at the level of discourse: straight pornographers speak of “cream pie” and the “internal pop,” whereas their gay counterparts speak of “barebacking” and describe internal ejaculation as “breeding.”
(page 170, emphasis of this interesting term is mine)

The difference in discourse may be an artifact of time. While I have little idea of what porncabulary looked like in 2009 on this book’s publication, today I think these terms are sufficiently cross-pollinated. In Pornhub’s 2025 stats, breeding doesn’t appear, but then neither does “raw” nor “bareback” (for straight consumption). While Pornhub doesn’t publish annual stats for their ‘gay’ section, they did publish stats on Pride 2026, covering June. None of these terms—raw, breeding, creampie, bareback—show up either. I’m relatively surprised that bareback doesn’t show up! The top results are not surprising: twink, femboy (talk about interesting discourse), anime, and the coveted “straight guys first time.” Only, when you get to the section, “Top Relative Gay Categories by Age Group,” do some of these terms appear. While 18-24 year olds are typing “cartoon” into the search bar, 35-44 year olds are typing “creampie.” Bareback shows up in the top 5 consistently in the older cohorts (45-54, 55-64, 65+). A 45-year-old in 2026 would have been born in 1981, would have been mid-puberty around 1996, mid-epidemic. Those older would have experienced much of the epidemic’s beginning, and almost certainly lost people to the AIDS epidemic.

Conclusion

I have two or three other sections that I’ve been tinkering with. Maybe they’ll get added to this, maybe not. They are/were about masculinity and the pornification/commodification of sex (thinking specifically about grindr and platforms like fansly/onlyfans). I’ve been tinkering with this for about two weeks since I finished the book and I can feel my steam going down, and I’m not sure I’ll finish those sections. When I began this, I wondered if it wouldn’t be something better for a separate article.

I’m planning to read Dean’s newer work, Hatred of Sex soon, and perhaps some of these threads will find a way to resolve there. In the meantime, I would suggest this book only to folks with a relatively academic interest in the dynamics of gay sex specific to barebacking in the late 00’s. Much of the topic is changed significantly with the widespread adoption of frequent testing and pre- and post-exposure prophylaxis. Certainly, there is a subculture around barebacking. People who like to fuck raw are all over the place, but I would only assign folks to that subculture that have a near-militant orientation towards barebacking, such that they lead with this in encounters, organize and attend groups focused specifically on barebacking for this sect of barebackers, etc. Someone with a casual aversion to the condom, especially when they are also taking PrEP/DoxyPEP and testing routinely, can hardly be called subcultural. Drawing these boundaries does not result in any such pathologization, but lets a population be better defined for better understanding.
